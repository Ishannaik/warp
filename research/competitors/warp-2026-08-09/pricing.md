## Value metric analysis

There is no value metric because there is no price. The closest thing to a
differentiating axis is **file size handling**, and even there every product
claims no hard limit. [Data]

The one real product boundary is Wormhole's: files **up to 5 GB are stored on
their servers, encrypted, for 24 hours**. Only larger transfers go direct.
[Data] That is a storage cost they are absorbing, and the most likely thing a
future paid tier would gate.

## What this means

**Do not build a paid tier.** [Opinion] The category norm is free, the
substitutes are free and open source, and the switching cost is opening a
different URL. A paid Warp tier would need to beat "just use PairDrop" for a
user who has already decided price is zero.

**The $0 constraint is not a disadvantage here.** Every competitor operates the
same way. What separates them is who absorbs relay or storage cost. Warp absorbs
neither, which is why it can promise no card will ever be charged and mean it.

## Whitespace

None on price. [Data]

The only unclaimed position is **trust that is checkable**: open source, no
relay, no storage, all three verifiable by reading the repo. ToffeeShare and
Wormhole cannot make that claim. PairDrop can, but routes through TURN when
hole-punching fails.
