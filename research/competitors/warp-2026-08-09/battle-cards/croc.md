# Battle card: croc

Generated 2026-08-09.

## Who they are

The CLI incumbent. 39,544 stars, MIT, released **v11.0.2 three days ago**, and
6 open issues. [Data, gh api 2026-08-09] Actively and tidily maintained.

## Why this card exists

croc is not a browser tool, so it looks adjacent rather than competitive. It is
listed here because losing to a default is the most common way a developer tool
dies, and for the technical audience that finds Warp through GitHub, croc is
already installed.

## Where they genuinely win

- **Resumable transfers**, which is the capability Warp is counting on as a
  differentiator.
- **No browser, no tab, no RAM ceiling.**
- **Scriptable**, so it goes in a cron job or a Makefile.
- **6 open issues at 39k stars.** That is a maintenance signal Warp cannot match
  with 126 open issues at 10 stars.

## Where they lose

- **Requires an install on both ends.** The person receiving your holiday photos
  will not install a Go binary.
- **Relays through a public server by default** unless both peers can connect
  directly.
- **No UI**, so it is unusable for a non-technical recipient.

## The honest answer to "why not just use croc"

If both people are comfortable in a terminal, use croc. It is mature, fast, and
better maintained than Warp.

Warp's case is the recipient. A link and a 6-character code works for someone
who has never opened a terminal, on a phone, with nothing installed. croc cannot
reach that person at all.

Do not position Warp against croc on capability. Position it on who can receive.
